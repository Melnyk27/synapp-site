Why not your editor's built-in sync?

Built-in waveform sync is fine for a few clips with clean sound. With four cameras, twenty takes and a recorder that ran for an hour straight, it turns into manual work.

  • Hundreds of clips at once — no picking pairs by hand.
  • Repeats and multiple events — each scene lands on its own, without false matches.
  • Drift on long recordings — corrected automatically.
  • One app for every editor — sync once, export wherever you cut.

Questions

Do I need timecode or a clapper?

No. TakeSync compares the sound recorded by different devices at the same time. 5–10 seconds of overlapping, audible sound is enough.

Which formats are supported?

MOV, MP4, MTS/M2TS, MXF, WAV, AIFF, MP3, M4A and more — anything ffmpeg can read. AVCHD, P2, XDCAM, DCIM and Canon XF card structures are recognised automatically.

What happens to a clip that doesn't sync?

It's marked red. Usually it was recorded when no other device was rolling, or the audio is too quiet. On export you can send such clips to the end of the timeline.

Does it work with Premiere Pro, Final Cut and Resolve?

Yes: XML for Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve, fcpxml for Final Cut Pro, plus new media files for any other editor.

Is it a subscription?

No. You pay once and the licence is yours, including every 1.x update.
